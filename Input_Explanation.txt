Importance of Inputs Used in the Crop Recommendation System

Your project uses different inputs to predict the best crop for a farmer. Each input is important because crop growth depends on soil condition, weather, location, and farming season.

1. Farmer

The farmer input helps identify who owns the prediction and allows the system to save records of recommendations.

Why it is important:

Helps track prediction history for each farmer.
Makes it easier to manage recommendations.

Example:
If Farmer Jean receives a maize recommendation today, the system can save it for future reference.

2. District

Different districts have different climates, rainfall, and environmental conditions.

Why it is important:

Helps the system recommend crops suitable for a specific location.
Climate conditions differ from one district to another.

Example:
A crop that grows well in Ngoma may not grow well in Musanze because of climate differences.

3. Altitude

Altitude is the height of an area above sea level. It affects temperature and rainfall.

Why it is important:

High-altitude areas are cooler.
Low-altitude areas are warmer.

Example:
Irish potatoes grow better in cool highland areas, while cassava grows better in warmer lowland areas.

4. Soil pH

Soil pH shows whether the soil is acidic or alkaline. It affects nutrient absorption by crops.

Why it is important:

Different crops grow well in different soil pH levels.
Poor soil pH can reduce crop growth.

Example:
Beans may grow better in slightly acidic soil.

5. Nitrogen (N)

Nitrogen helps plants grow healthy leaves and stems.

Why it is important:

Supports plant growth and greenness.

Example:
Maize needs enough nitrogen to grow tall and healthy.

6. Phosphorus (P)

Phosphorus supports strong root growth and seed development.

Why it is important:

Helps plants develop stronger roots.
Improves crop production.

Example:
Beans and maize need phosphorus for healthy growth.

7. Potassium (K)

Potassium helps plants resist diseases and manage water properly.

Why it is important:

Helps crops survive dry conditions.
Improves plant strength.

Example:
Potassium helps crops stay healthy during hot weather.

8. Temperature

Temperature affects crop growth and development.

Why it is important:

Each crop grows best under a certain temperature range.

Example:
Potatoes prefer cooler temperatures, while maize grows better in warmer conditions.

9. Humidity

Humidity means the amount of moisture in the air.

Why it is important:

Affects plant health and disease levels.

Example:
Very high humidity can sometimes increase crop diseases.

10. Season

Season is an important factor because crops grow differently depending on the farming period.

In Rwanda, there are three agricultural seasons:

Season A: September – February
Season B: March – June
Season C: July – September

Why it is important:

Some crops grow better in certain seasons because of rainfall and weather conditions.
Helps the system recommend crops suitable for the current farming season.

Example:
Beans and maize may perform well in Season A or B, while vegetables and sweet potatoes are often suitable for Season C, especially in irrigated areas.

Conclusion

All these inputs help the system understand the farmer’s location, soil condition, weather, and farming season. By combining this information, the AI system can recommend the best crop for a farmer, helping improve productivity and reduce farming risks.
